# Desk Note — A Split Tape in the Build-Out (Aug 7, 2026)

**Bottom line.** Calm tape, uptrend intact — but this is a *selection market*: four of the six busiest fields are splitting into winners and losers rather than moving together, so beta is quiet and name-picking is doing the work. The one story worth your week is the AI build-out complex — electric utilities and construction contractors — where the *equities* are visibly stressed while their *physical inputs* disagree. Copper and steel are ripping as the contractor stocks crack, and uranium has bled for a quarter even as the "power-demand" utility names hold. That contradiction is the most useful question on the board.

## The backdrop
- **Volatility: 14.9, 21st percentile — calm** (reading is 3 days old). Low-vol, risk-on behavior; the dispersions below read as idiosyncratic, not panic.
- **Trend: +0.19, uptrend — intact but aging** (reading is 40 days old). Still up; treat the freshness with a grain of salt.
- **Valuation: 59th percentile — middling-to-slightly-rich, but the input is ~3 years stale** (1,074 days). Effectively ignore it; do not anchor on it.

## Where to look this week

**1) The build-out complex — power + construction (STRESS).** Three of the top six fields cluster here: Engineering & Construction (STRESS, −4.8%/21d), Electric Utilities: Central (STRESS, −2.7%), and the broad Utilities group (dispersion). The equities are cracking and splitting — LMB −37%, AGX −13% among contractors; NRG −16%, FISN −19% among utilities — while a few narrative winners hold (CEG +8%, IESC +19%).

*Physical cross-check — the tension.* Copper (COPX **+16.8%**/21d) and steel (SLX **+13.3%**) — the raw inputs to every data center and substation — are ripping, and the broad infrastructure basket (PAVE +4.0%) is up, not down. So the contractor selloff looks **narrow and company-specific, not an end-demand collapse**; the metals say the physical build-out is still on. The *power* leg is weaker, though: uranium (URA) is +6.0%/21d but **−20.2%/63d**, and nuclear (NLR) −18.3% over the quarter — the "AI needs nuclear" utility leadership is narrowing to a handful of names with no physical tailwind behind it.

*Research question.* Is the E&C drawdown idiosyncratic — pull LMB/AGX backlog and guidance, and non-residential construction spend — or the first crack? Watch COPX/SLX: if copper rolls over, the demand story changes. For power, check uranium *term* pricing and actual signed data-center power-purchase agreements (PPAs), not the CEG headline.

**2) Finance / Investor Services — BDCs (DISPERSION, rising).** The single highest-attention field (0.90) and the counter-tone to the stress cluster: it is *rising* (+5.2%/21d) and splitting — Main Street (MAIN) +14%, CION +20% vs. flat KYN/NMCO. These are business-development companies (BDCs): public vehicles that lend to mid-size private firms, so they are a live read on private-credit health.

*Cross-check.* Regional banks (KRE) are **+9.0%/63d** — credit is not visibly breaking, which argues the BDC strength is benign winners-winning, not distress.

*Research question.* Into BDC earnings, are net asset values (NAV) holding and non-accruals (loans going bad) staying low? If KRE and BDC NAVs turn down together, that is the first real credit-crack signal.

**The honest caveat.** Don't over-read this. Five of the six top fields carry *depressed volume-influx* — the dispersion is happening on thin participation, not a stampede of fresh money. And the two smallest "hot" fields, Advertising (n=7) and Packaged Foods (n=23), are single-stock events wearing a field costume — WPP +59% and UTZ +83% are doing the work. Treat them as names, below, not themes.

- **Vol break above ~20** — flips this from a stock-picker's tape into a correlated, beta-driven one.
- **KRE or BDC NAVs turning down** — first genuine sign private credit is cracking.
