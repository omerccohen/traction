## Where to look this week

**1) Memory & storage chips — and the equipment behind them (the main event).**
Three of the six flagged groups point at the same thing. Inside tech, the split is violent: memory/storage makers **SanDisk (-35%) and Western Digital (-25%)** are getting crushed while the rest of tech rips (Gartner +40%, Zebra +38%, Microsoft +30%). That's *dispersion* — the gap between a group's winners and losers blowing wide open. Meanwhile **chip-equipment makers KLA (-14%) and Lam Research (-12%)** are falling on elevated volatility — our "stress" signal — even as chip-*testing* gear (Teradyne) holds up.

The connective tissue: KLA and Lam live or die on how much memory makers spend building new factories. When memory prices roll over, memory makers slam the brakes on equipment orders. So a memory-price problem and an equipment-stock problem tend to be the *same* problem, one step apart. The pain sitting in capacity-adding equipment (not test gear) fits that read.

*The question to go answer:* Are DRAM and NAND memory prices actually rolling over? Check **contract memory pricing** (more telling than spot), plus capex-cut signals from the big memory makers — Micron, Samsung, SK Hynix — and wafer-equipment (WFE) spending forecasts. If contract prices are falling and capex guidance is coming down, this has legs and a reason. If it's spot-price noise, it may be a headline overreaction.

**2) Insurance is quietly lighting up.**
Unusually, two of the six active groups are insurance: **property & casualty insurers** and **insurance brokers**. Both show extreme dispersion — Travelers +14% and broker Willis +19% up, Progressive -6% lagging. Insurance is normally a sleepy corner; when it clusters like this, it's usually the **pricing cycle** turning.

*The question to go answer:* Is the P&C market "hardening" (insurers pushing rates up) or "softening" (competition eroding them)? Check the just-completed mid-year **reinsurance renewals**, catastrophe-season loss trends, and the commercial-lines (Travelers) vs. auto (Progressive) split — the divergence smells like a commercial-vs-auto story.

*One trap:* Specialty Chemicals posts the **highest** raw attention score — but its character is "quiet, being ignored," not stressed. High score, low urgency. Skip it.
