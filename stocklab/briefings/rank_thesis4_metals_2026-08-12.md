# Thesis 4 — Metals & Mining: Who Actually Has Unhedged Leverage to the Copper Concentrate Squeeze?

**Date:** 2026-08-12 (filings through Q2 2026, filed 2026-07-22 → 2026-08-12)
**Question tested:** The Metal Mining field is +20.2%/21d while volume influx sits at the 24th percentile. Which of these 22 names actually owns unhedged, producing, growing exposure to the verified physical tightness — and which are wearing the field's costume?

**This is a positioning assessment, not a price prediction and not advice. Direction is unpredictable.**

---

## Is this move funded or is it a squeeze?

**Verdict: FUNDED where the copper actually is; squeeze-flavoured where it is not.** The two halves of the field are being bought by different people for different reasons, and the field statistic averages them into one misleading number.

**1. Short interest ROSE through the rally in the three most liquid copper producers.** You cannot squeeze a book that is growing.

| Name | Short interest | Direction through the rally | 21d move |
|---|---|---|---|
| **FCX** | 27.1–27.9M shares, **~1.9–2.0%** of shares out | **Rose** from 24.93M to 27.87M ([Finviz](https://finviz.com/quote?t=FCX&ty=si&p=d&r=m6), [MarketBeat](https://www.marketbeat.com/stocks/NYSE/FCX/short-interest)) | +15% |
| **HBM** | 9.5M shares, **2.5%** of float | **Rose 24.0%** vs the prior reporting period ([Fintel](https://fintel.io/ss/us/hbm)) | +33% |
| **ERO** | 4.13M shares, **4.37%** of float | **Rose** from 3.4% of float at 15 Jun 2026 ([Fintel](https://fintel.io/ss/us/ero), [ChartMill](https://www.chartmill.com/stock/quote/ERO/profile)) | +47% |

Shorts were *adding* into the move and being run over. The marginal buyer paid up against increasing short supply. That is funded buying, not covering.

**2. The genuinely crowded shorts are in the names with NO copper.** Short interest is concentrated exactly where the commodity exposure is wrong:

- **CLF ~13.51% of float** (early July 2026, [GuruFocus](https://www.gurufocus.com/term/ShortPercentageOfFloat/CLF)) — a **steel producer**, +28%/21d, running a **$(145)M quarterly net loss**.
- **IE ~11.88% of float** (10,302,852 shares, [Finviz](https://finviz.com/quote.ashx?t=IE)) — a **pre-revenue explorer**, +26%/21d.
- **MP Materials and Cleveland-Cliffs together "lead short interest among materials stocks above $2B"** ([Seeking Alpha](https://seekingalpha.com/news/4609511-mp-materials-cleveland-cliffs-lad-short-interest-among-materials-stocks-above-2b)) — MP is a **rare-earth** company with a contractual price floor.

By contrast **ASM sits at just 2.47% of float** with 5.11 days to cover as of 10 Jul 2026 ([Fintel](https://fintel.io/ss/us/asm)) — its +29% is a *silver* move, not a squeeze.

**3. The money is going into the wrapper and the metal, which is why it does not show up as per-name equity turnover.** COPX pulled roughly **$1.7B of net inflows to ~$7.5B AUM — about four times its assets six months earlier** ([etf.com](https://www.etf.com/sections/features/coppers-breakout-fueling-surge-copx)). ETF creations buy the whole basket at once; that is price impact without a corresponding spike in individual-name participation.

**4. Why the volume percentile is mechanically depressed — the field is not one field.** Computed on the 22 names here using the roster's caps and volumes (`data_cache/universe/all_us_stocks.csv`):

- Simple-average 21d return **+17.3%**, dollar-volume-weighted **+14.8%**, cap-weighted **+12.4%**. **The move is biggest in the smallest names.**
- The field's traded dollars sit overwhelmingly in names with no copper: **MP ~$631M/day** (rare earths), **USAR ~$367M/day** (pre-revenue rare earths), **VALE ~$357M/day** (iron ore), **CLF ~$155M/day** (steel). The copper producers that actually moved trade far less: **HBM ~$135M/day, ERO ~$94M/day**.
- Of these 22 SIC-1000 "Metal Mining" filers, **only four are copper-majority producers** (FCX, SCCO, ERO, HBM). The other eighteen are gold, silver, iron ore, steel, zinc, lithium, rare earths and tungsten. A field-wide "volume influx" percentile is averaging conviction in four names against apathy in eighteen.

**5. The filings independently confirm the physical squeeze — four separate companies, four separate ways.** This is the strongest evidence in the whole review, because none of it is commentary:

- **FCX**: treatment-charge deduction from revenue fell to **$8M from $16M** (Q2) and **$10M from $43M** (H1), "primarily reflect[ing] **lower treatment charge rates as a result of favorable market conditions**" ([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/831259/000083125926000036/fcx-20260630.htm)).
- **HBM**: the treatment-and-refining-charges line turned into a **+$0.1M credit** in Q2 2026 against $(3.3)M a year earlier; H1 $(3.0)M vs $(17.3)M ([MD&A](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1322422/000106299326003907/exhibit99-2.htm)).
- **ERO**: "negotiated **improved copper smelting and refining terms, expected to deliver more than $20 million in savings**"; Q2 costs held down by "a **favorable decrease in smelting and refining charge terms** at the Caraíba Operations" ([MD&A](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1853860/000162828026053508/erocopper-mdax2026q2.htm)).
- **VALE**: "TC/RCs, penalties, premiums and discounts" fell to **US$100/t from US$218/t, −54% y/y** ([2Q26 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/917851/000129281426004002/valepr2q26_6k.htm)).

Spot TC/RCs at **−$126.80/t** against a **$0/t record-low annual benchmark** set in January 2026 ([Crux Investor](https://www.cruxinvestor.com/posts/chinese-smelters-pay-miners-for-copper-concentrate-as-mine-supply-becomes-the-key-constraint)) is not a narrative. It is showing up in four sets of audited-adjacent numbers.

### The counter-evidence I am not suppressing

**The ICSG premise in the brief is stale, and the correction matters.** The ICSG forecast a **150,000t refined deficit for 2026** in October 2025 ([Mining Engineering](https://me.smenet.org/slower-production-growth-will-push-copper-market-to-deficit-in-2026-says-icsg/), [The Oregon Group](https://theoregongroup.com/commodities/copper/international-copper-study-group-warns-of-150000t-deficit-in-2026/)) — but **in April 2026 it revised to a ~96,000t refined SURPLUS**, cutting refined-use growth to 1.6% from 2.1% as secondary/scrap output filled the gap, with refined production growth of just 0.4% ([Mining.com](https://www.mining.com/web/refined-copper-output-growth-to-slow-in-2026-icsg/), [Recycling Today](https://www.recyclingtoday.com/news/copper-icsg-recycling-output-refining-supply-demand-2026-2027/)). **The verified tightness is in CONCENTRATE, not in refined metal.** That makes miners the beneficiaries and smelters the victims — which is exactly what the four filings above show — but it removes the "refined deficit" leg from the price argument. Treat the concentrate squeeze as HIGH confidence and the refined balance as contested.

**Volume is falling at the two largest names.** FCX Q2 copper sales **710 Mlb vs 1,016 Mlb (−30% y/y)** and production **786 vs 963 Mlb (−18%)**; SCCO copper sold **−1.5%** and produced **−3.4%**. Part of this price is scarcity the producers created with their own outages. That is bullish for price and bearish for the volume half of the leverage equation.

**Costs are rising at the biggest name.** FCX unit net cash costs **$1.97/lb vs $1.13 (+74% y/y)**, plus a further $0.40/lb of excluded idle-facility costs.

**Bottom line on the anomaly: this is an early re-rating that has been funded — but funded narrowly, through the index wrapper and into four producers, while eighteen unrelated names ride the label.** The low participation reading is measuring the eighteen.

## Company detail — verified vs inferred

**SCCO — 91%.** VERIFIED from the 10-Q: copper $3,116.8M of $4,289.0M (72.7%), copper+moly+zinc 87.3%, and a derivatives note that contains *only* provisionally-priced embedded derivatives explicitly "**Not classified as hedges**" — there is no forward sales book of any kind. The decisive number is the cost line: operating cash cost net of by-product revenues of **$0.05/lb in Q2 and negative $(0.03)/lb for H1**, against $0.63 and $0.70 a year earlier, which proves the by-product and treatment-charge tailwind in a single figure. It ranks first because net sales +40.6% against operating costs +13.8% is the widest verified price-to-cost gap in the set. The honest caveat, also verified, is that volumes are **not** rising (copper sold −1.5%, produced −3.4%) — this is a pure price-and-margin story, not a volume story.

**FCX — 84%.** VERIFIED: copper is 76.9% of Q2 revenue and the hedge book is trivial — 145 Mlb of futures and swaps at $5.95/lb against 3,057 Mlb of guided 2026 sales, disclosed as a pass-through for customers who "request a fixed market price." INFERRED (arithmetic from disclosed components): that is ~4.7% of a year's sales, so roughly 95% of the book prices at spot. FCX ranks second rather than first on two verified negatives that SCCO does not share: sales volumes fell **30% y/y** and unit net cash costs rose **74% y/y** to $1.97/lb before a further $0.40/lb of excluded idle-facility costs. Its unique offsetting merit is expressibility — **~$785M/day of turnover**, the deepest liquidity in the field, and a disclosed sensitivity of **$150M of operating cash flow per $0.10/lb**. The Indonesian IUPK extension, applied for in June 2026 and still ungranted, is the single largest binary in this table.

**HBM — 79%.** VERIFIED: copper 50.7% of Q2 revenue, realized copper $6.13/lb against an LME QTD of $6.05, cash-cost guidance improved to $(0.45)–$(0.25)/lb, and net debt of **negative** $80.5M. The TC/RC line flipping to a small credit is the cleanest single-line proof in these filings that negative treatment charges reach the income statement. It ranks below ERO on commodity purity and above it on balance sheet: HBM is net cash while ERO carries $452.7M of net debt against $181.7M of liquidity. It ranks below FCX because **~40% of revenue is gold**, and the company itself attributes the cost improvement to "strong gold by-product credits" — meaning a material share of what reads as copper margin is really gold leverage that would reverse with the gold price.

**ERO — 74%.** VERIFIED and structurally the most elegant: the *only* hedge programme is FX (≈70% of operating and capital costs protected at a USD/BRL floor of 5.54), which insulates the cost base while leaving the copper price completely open. Volumes are verifiably rising — Tucumã H1 17,425t vs 11,418t — and guidance was maintained rather than cut. It ranks below HBM because three separate cost and capital metrics moved the wrong way in this same filing: gold C1/AISC guidance was **raised**, gold production was guided to the **low end**, and total capex guidance was **raised to $285–330M**, all against $452.7M of net debt and only $181.7M of total liquidity. After +47% in 21 days it is also the most-priced name here.

**RIO — 62%.** VERIFIED: copper underlying EBITDA of $5.7B, +84% y/y, is 36% of the $15.8B reportable-segment total, with the CEO stating that copper, aluminium and lithium together now exceed 50% of underlying EBITDA. The productivity programme is quantified and real — $870M banked year-to-date toward a $1.8B annualised run-rate — so cost direction is genuinely favourable. It ranks below the four pure names because **iron ore is still the largest segment at $6.8B and it is flat (−1%)**, and because Simandou commits multi-billion-dollar capital to *more* iron ore supply. Its copper leverage is real and growing but structurally diluted; this is the best risk-adjusted expression in the set and the worst pure one.

**VALE — 41%.** VERIFIED: realized copper $14,062/t (+57%), copper sales +10%, all-in copper cost of **negative** US$257/t, and 2026 all-in copper cost guidance cut by roughly $1,000/t. Also verified is the thing that caps it: copper is **14.8% of revenue** against Iron Ore Solutions at 75%, and attributable net income **fell 35% y/y**. INFERRED: on segment EBITDA alone (Iron Ore ~$3.06B + Base Metals $1.29B), copper is about 24% of the group's earning power. The Salobo gold stream is a partial forward sale of the very by-product credit driving the negative cost — the filing itself says break-even would rise to US$3,378/t on a cash-effect basis. It ranks below RIO because RIO's copper is a larger share of a faster-improving book.

**BVN — 36%.** VERIFIED: US$117.5M of Cerro Verde dividends received on 24 July and US$274.1M year-to-date, plus the $215.7M gap between EBITDA including affiliates and EBITDA from direct operations. That is genuine copper cash. **NEXA — 33%.** VERIFIED and the most interesting counter-case in the set: adjusted EBITDA +78% with margin +8.8pp is real base-metals margin expansion, and zinc volumes are rising (+8% Q2, +13% H1). Its smelting segment's margin expansion is attributed to "**higher treatment charges**" — it earns what the miners pay — and the 2026 zinc benchmark TC **rose** to US$85/t from US$80/t, the mirror image of copper's −$126.80/t spot. ## Method + caveats

**What I did.** For all 22 names I resolved CIK from `company_tickers.json`, pulled `data.sec.gov/submissions/`, and read the actual primary documents: 10-Qs for FCX, SCCO, CLF, IE, MP, USAR, DC, NB and ALOY; 6-K exhibits (press release + MD&A) for HBM, ERO, VALE, NEXA, BVN, AUGO, RIO and GMTL. Every hard number attributed to a filing above was extracted from the filing text, not from a summary. Market caps and dollar volumes come from the repo's own roster, `data_cache/universe/all_us_stocks.csv`.

**Foreign private issuers — filing form confirmed, not guessed.** **RIO, VALE, BVN, NEXA, SGML, CRML** and **GMTL** file **20-F annually and 6-K interim** (no 10-Q exists). **HBM, ERO, ASM, USAS, LAR** and **AUGO** are also 6-K filers. **FCX, SCCO, CLF, IE, MP, USAR, DC, NB** and **ALOY** file 10-Q. All 22 do file with the SEC; none had to be inferred. TX was named in the brief's method note but is **not in the ranked list** and was not researched.

**LOW-confidence items, flagged explicitly:**
- **All short-interest figures are third-party aggregator data** (Fintel, Finviz, MarketBeat, GuruFocus, ChartMill) surfaced through search, not primary FINRA/exchange files. Sources disagree at the margin (FCX 1.89% vs 1.95%; ERO 4.37% of float vs 4.52% of shares out; ASM 2.47% vs 0.33%). **The direction of change is the load-bearing claim and it is consistent across sources; the absolute levels are LOW confidence.** I could not fetch the Seeking Alpha MP/CLF short-interest article directly (egress-blocked), so that datapoint rests on a search summary alone.
- **The copper price level is not internally reconciled.** The FCX 10-Q states the **LME** settlement price closed at an **all-time high of $6.56/lb on 6 August 2026**. The brief's **~$6.90/lb** is presumably COMEX, which carries a tariff premium (COMEX averaged $6.16 vs LME $6.05 in Q2 per both FCX and SCCO). I verified $6.56 LME from a filing; **I did not verify $6.90 anywhere** — treat it as LOW confidence.
- **The ICSG premise in the brief is contradicted by my own sourcing.** The 2026 refined **deficit** of 150kt was the **October 2025** forecast; **in April 2026 the ICSG revised to a ~96kt refined SURPLUS**. The concentrate squeeze (−$126.80/t spot, $0/t benchmark) is HIGH confidence and confirmed inside four separate filings; **the refined-market deficit is CONTESTED and should not be relied on.**
- **BVN's revenue split by metal is unverified** — the 6-K is a summary release and the full earnings report was not obtainable. Its copper weighting is inferred from production changes and the affiliates/direct EBITDA gap.
- **SGML's Q2 2026 financials do not exist yet** (release scheduled 14 August 2026). Only production is verified.
- **NB and ALOY have no June-quarter filing on EDGAR** — their most recent periodic reports cover 31 March 2026. Both are ranked on stale financials, which is part of why they rank low.
- **COPX flow data ($1.7B inflows, ~$7.5B AUM)** comes from an etf.com feature whose most detailed figures date to **February 2026**. Direct fetches of globalxetfs.com, stockanalysis.com, investing.com, seekingalpha.com and discoveryalert.com.au were **egress-blocked**, so I could not obtain a current AUM print. **The direction (large, sustained inflows) is well-supported; the August 2026 level is unverified.**

**Companies mislabeled in this field.** **CLF** is a steel producer carrying legacy SIC 1000 from its iron-ore past — it is 100% steel by revenue and consumes base metals as inputs. **ALOY** is a development-stage rare-earth/magnet company that reached Nasdaq via reverse merger with a stock-trading software business and mines nothing. Both materially distort any Metal Mining field aggregate and should be excluded from it. Beyond outright mislabeling, **fourteen of the twenty-two names have no meaningful copper revenue**: twelve have literally none (CLF, AUGO, MP, LAR, SGML, NB, USAR, DC, CRML, ALOY, GMTL, and IE — which has no revenue of any kind), and two have token amounts that round to nothing (ASM at ~331t and USAS at ~68t of copper for the quarter). Only **four are copper-majority producers** — FCX, SCCO, ERO, HBM. The "Metal Mining +20.2%" reading is therefore measuring a taxonomy far more than it is measuring copper.

**What would most change this ranking.** (1) The **Q4 2026 copper concentrate benchmark negotiation** — if TC/RCs revert toward positive, the entire mechanism unwinds regardless of the copper price. (2) **Indonesia granting or refusing FCX's IUPK extension.** (3) **Two consecutive quarters of rising sales volumes at FCX and SCCO** — that would convert this from a price-and-margin story into genuine volume leverage and would justify moving both scores up. (4) A **Section 232 refined-copper decision** in advance of January 2027.
