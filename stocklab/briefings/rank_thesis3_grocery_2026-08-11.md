# Thesis 3 — Food Chains: which businesses are DURABLE through the current pressure (as of 2026-08-11)

**Question being scored:** not price, not a buy call — *the probability each company's business stays durable through the pressure now hitting the group*. Higher = defensible niche, holding traffic/units, stable margins, safe balance sheet, guidance intact. Lower = losing volume, compressing margin, cutting guidance, levered.

**Data vintage:** the most recent filing each company has actually made, retrieved from SEC EDGAR 2026-08-11/12. Fresh Q2/Q3 data for KR (Q1 FY26), ACI (Q1 FY26), SFM, ARKO, NGVC, IMKTA. **GO and TBBB both report tonight (Aug 12) and YSWY has not filed Q2 — those three are flagged LOW-confidence.**

---

## What is actually driving the stress

**It is not food input costs. The filings say so explicitly, and they name three other things instead: (1) a ~30% year-over-year spike in retail gasoline prices that is draining the household grocery wallet, (2) the Inflation Reduction Act's Medicare drug-price program stripping 130–150bp of comp out of the pharmacy-heavy chains, and (3) unit/traffic erosion that management is masking with price.**

**1. The input-cost hypothesis is falsified in management's own words.** Kroger's Q1 FY26 gross-margin bridge lists **"egg deflation"** — *deflation* — as a headwind, quantified at **64 basis points** of the ID-sales calculation, alongside "higher transportation costs, and planned price investments" ([KR 8-K ex-99.1, 6/18/26](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/56873/000110465926075395/tm2618219d1_ex99-1.htm); [KR 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/56873/000110465926078236/kr-20260523x10q.htm)). Yesway's 10-Q decomposes its own merchandise margin expansion as **average selling price per unit +4.1% against average cost per unit +1.0%** — i.e. product cost inflation running ~1%, with retailers taking 4% of price on top ([YSWY 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1859836/000110465926069252/tmb-20260331x10q.htm)). NGVC attributes its 60bp gross-margin decline to **"unfavorable change in sales mix, as well as higher merchandise inventory shrink and freight costs"** — mix, shrink, freight; not commodity cost ([NGVC 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1547459/000143774926026255/ngvc20260630_10q.htm)). **DBA being flat is consistent with the filings, not in tension with them.**

**2. The physical driver the agriculture ETF cannot see is at the pump.** Kroger: *"Total supermarket fuel sales increased 21.3%... primarily due to an increase in the average retail fuel price of 22.7%"* ([KR 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/56873/000110465926078236/kr-20260523x10q.htm)). Albertsons: total revenue rose 0.2% but *"the increase in Net sales and other revenue was primarily driven by higher fuel sales, while identical sales declined 0.8%"* ([ACI Q1 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1646972/000164697226000041/aciq126earningsrelease.htm)). ARKO's CEO states the mechanism outright: **"Consumer demand softened during the second quarter as sustained higher fuel prices continued to pressure household budgets"** ([ARKO 8-K ex-99.1, 8/7/26](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1823794/000119312526339092/arko-ex99_1.htm)). ARKO also books **"a $3.3 million increase in credit card fees driven by higher fuel prices"** as a direct opex hit. Yesway independently attributes its fuel-margin windfall to wholesale price volatility *"influenced by recent geopolitical developments in the Middle East."* Externally: the US national average hit **$4.10/gal on Aug 3, 2026, +30.0% year over year, with every state up at least 10%** ([LendingTree gas price study](https://www.lendingtree.com/credit-cards/study/us-gas-prices/)). Energy, not agriculture, is taking the marginal grocery dollar — which is exactly why a food-commodity proxy shows nothing.

**3. A second, purely regulatory driver hits the two largest names and nothing else.** Kroger's FY26 ID-sales guidance *"includes approximately 130 basis points unfavorable impact from the Inflation Reduction Act"*; Albertsons' updated outlook *"reflects an estimated 150 basis point headwind related to the Inflation Reduction Act's Medicare Drug Price Negotiation Program which took effect on January 1, 2026."* That is a pharmacy price cut showing up inside a grocery comp line. It is roughly the entire difference between KR's +1.0% and ACI's −0.8%, and it is structural, not cyclical. **It also explains why the cluster is co-moving without a commodity cause.**

**4. Underneath all of it: units and traffic are falling, and price is doing the work.** The cleanest disclosure is Kroger's — ID sales rose *"due to... increased spend per item, partially offset by a reduction in the number of units sold."* NGVC's +1.2% comp is **+3.1% transaction size less 1.8% transaction count**, with traffic deteriorating (nine-month count −0.6% vs Q3 −1.8%). Ingles' grocery comps **ex-fuel were −3.1%** while headline sales rose 1.6% on fuel. Albertsons' own words: *"core grocery faced increasing pressure from softer industry unit trends and a more cautious consumer."*

**Hypotheses tested and NOT supported:**
- **SNAP / food-stamp changes:** searched every filing. SNAP appears **only** in boilerplate risk-factor lists (GO's forward-looking-statements paragraph; ACI's "shifts in state and federal assistance programs"; KR's "changes in government-funded benefit programs"). **No company attributes any current result to it.** Not a driver on this evidence.
- **GLP-1 weight-loss drugs:** **zero mentions** across all nine companies' latest filings. No support whatsoever.
- **Fuel-margin collapse at the c-stores:** the **opposite** is true. ARKO's retail same-store fuel margin **rose to 48.7 cpg from 45.7 cpg** and it *raised* its full-year fuel-margin outlook. Yesway's fuel margin went **49.4 cpg from 35.9 cpg**. Fuel margin is these companies' crutch, not their wound — their wound is gallons (ARKO same-store gallons **−5.7%**).
- **Input-cost inflation:** falsified above.
- **Share loss to Walmart/Costco/Aldi:** partially supported but as a *response*, not a cause — KR cites "planned price investments," ACI "incremental investments in its customer value proposition," GO "product promotions to drive sales." The industry is discounting into a volume decline, which compresses margin. TBBB (a hard discounter) posting **+16.0% same-store sales** is the mirror image of the same trade-down.

**The one company-specific event that produced the biggest drawdown is a guidance cut, and its stated reason is volume:** ACI cut FY26 adjusted EBITDA by **$300M (−7.8%)** and adjusted EPS by **~21%**, with ID-sales guidance moved from **0.0%/+1.0% to −1.5%/−0.5%**, because of *"continued softness in industry unit trends and a more cautious consumer."*

## Per-company evidence (VERIFIED = quoted from the cited filing; *inferred* = my arithmetic)

**1. KR — 85% (Kroger).** VERIFIED ([8-K 6/18/26](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/56873/000110465926075395/tm2618219d1_ex99-1.htm), [10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/56873/000110465926078236/kr-20260523x10q.htm)): ID sales ex-fuel **+1.0%**, adjusted EPS **$1.58 vs $1.49**, adjusted FIFO operating profit **$1,544M vs $1,518M**, eCommerce **+19%**, and full-year guidance **reaffirmed** at ID 1.0–2.0% / EPS $5.10–5.30 / FCF $2.7–2.9B. The durability case is the balance sheet and the composition of the headwind: net total debt/adjusted EBITDA is **1.75x against a 2.30–2.50x target**, and of the ~230bp of comp deceleration, **130bp is IRA pharmacy pricing, 64bp is egg deflation and 40bp is a brand-to-generic prescription shift** — three effects that are structural or transitory, not share loss. The honest ding, and the reason this isn't 90%: the 10-Q says ID sales rose on *"increased spend per item, partially offset by a reduction in the number of units sold"* — real volume is contracting, and gross margin fell to **22.7% from 23.0%** partly on *"planned price investments."* Kroger is paying with margin to hold the customer, and it has the most room in the group to do so.

**2. SFM — 80% (Sprouts).** VERIFIED ([8-K 7/29/26](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1575515/000157551526000036/sfm-20260628xex991.htm), [10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1575515/000157551526000037/sfm-20260628.htm)): comps **(1.0)%** versus **+10.2%** a year ago, but sequentially better than Q1's (1.7)%, and EPS of **$1.37 beat its own $1.32–$1.36 guide**. Gross margin was **38.7% vs 38.8% — the most stable margin in the group** — and the balance sheet is net cash (**$224M, zero drawn on $600M**) while repurchasing $70M in the quarter. Guidance was effectively held: FY sales growth was *raised* to 5.5–6.5% (from 4.5–6.5%) and the comp range narrowed to (0.5)–0.5%, with only the EBIT and EPS top ends trimmed ($695M→$685M; $5.48→$5.40) ([Q1 8-K for the prior guide](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1575515/000157551526000020/sfm-20260329xex991.htm)). Held at 80% rather than higher because a comp that swings 1,120bp in a year is a real demand signal, EBIT margin fell 60bp to 7.5%, and **Sprouts does not disclose traffic vs ticket** — so I cannot verify whether its comp is price-held like NGVC's and KR's almost certainly are.

**3. TBBB — 76% (BBB Foods / Tiendas 3B) — LOW-CONFIDENCE (stale).** VERIFIED for Q1 ([6-K 5/6/26](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1978954/000119312526209052/6-k_tbbb_1q26_earnings_r.htm)): **same-store sales +16.0%**, revenue **+33.4%**, 123 net new stores to 3,469, EBITDA ex non-cash share-based payment **Ps.1,276M, +38.9%** — achieved, in the CEO's words, *"despite a soft consumer environment in Mexico."* This is the one business in the set that is a *beneficiary* of the force hitting the others: a hard discounter gaining share as consumers trade down, with no US pharmacy/IRA exposure and no US fuel-retail exposure. Three real caveats keep it below SFM: the data is **four months old and Q2 prints tonight**; *reported* EBITDA fell year over year (Ps.554M vs Ps.705M) on a Ps.722M non-cash share-based charge, which is a governance/dilution cost even if non-cash; and the May follow-on at $32.50 was **12.6M of 13.3M shares sold by existing holders** — $409.6M to selling shareholders against $22.75M to the company ([pricing 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1978954/000119312526246212/trilogy_-_pricing_press_.htm)).

**4. IMKTA — 68% (Ingles Markets).** VERIFIED ([10-Q 8/6/26](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/50493/000005049326000020/imkt-20260627x10q.htm), [8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/50493/000143774926026048/ex_999422.htm)): the balance sheet is the best here — **cash $455.1M against total debt $500.5M**, stockholders' equity $1.68B, and only a **$0.9M letter of credit** outstanding on its $150M line. **8. **9. ARKO — 30% (ARKO Corp).** VERIFIED ([8-K ex-99.1 8/7/26](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1823794/000119312526339092/arko-ex99_1.htm), [10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1823794/000119312526339096/arko-20260630.htm)): the fuel-margin-collapse hypothesis is **wrong here — margins expanded** (retail same-store **48.7 cpg vs 45.7**; merchandise margin **34.7% vs 33.6%, +110bp**). The equity de-rating is about earnings quality, not near-term solvency.

## Does ARKO even belong in this peer group?

**No — and neither does YSWY. Both are fuel retailers wearing a grocery label, and the driver's *sign is inverted* for them.** For a grocer, a 30% jump in pump prices is a pure wallet drain: Ingles' food comps go to −3.1% while its fuel dollars flatter the headline. For ARKO and Yesway, the *same* input is simultaneously a revenue mix benefit (cpg expansion: 45.7→48.7 and 35.9→49.4) and a volume tax (ARKO same-store gallons −5.7%). A screen keying on "food retail" will keep pairing them with grocers and keep getting the causality backwards.

Two qualifications keep it from being a clean "remove them." First, the *consumer* leg is genuinely shared — ARKO's merchandise comp of (1.7)% and its CEO's "household budgets" quote rhyme exactly with what the grocers report, so the demand signal is common even though the margin signal is opposite. Second, ARKO's merchandise contribution ($120.5M) actually exceeded its retail fuel contribution ($99.5M) in Q2, so it is not *purely* a fuel name. **Practical recommendation: split the cluster into "grocery volume/IRA" (KR, ACI, SFM, IMKTA, NGVC, GO, TBBB) and "fuel-margin retail" (ARKO, YSWY), and drive the second off cents-per-gallon and gallons, not comps.** ARKO's −46% is a fuel-margin-dependence de-rating, not a grocery de-rating, and pooling it into a food-chain field average distorts the field.

## Method & caveats

- **What this is:** a fundamental, filings-based *positioning* read on business durability. **Not a price prediction and not investment advice.** Nothing here says "buy" or "will go up." The supplied 21d/63d price moves were context only and were **not** used to rank — note that the top-ranked name (KR, −5%/−13%) is down and a mid-ranked one (GO, −1%/+25%) is up.
- **Sources:** SEC EDGAR submissions API plus the actual latest 10-Q/10-K/8-K exhibit 99.1 (and 6-K for the foreign private issuer TBBB) for each company, retrieved 2026-08-11/12 with a descriptive User-Agent. Every hard number is linked inline. One external source is used, for the gasoline price level (LendingTree study citing a national average of $4.10/gal on 8/3/26, +30.0% y/y) — that is a **secondary source and is LOW-confidence as to precise magnitude**, though the *direction* is independently corroborated inside three separate filings (KR's +22.7% average retail fuel price, ACI's "higher fuel sales," ARKO's "sustained higher fuel prices").
- **LOW-confidence flags:** **GO** and **TBBB** both report Q2 after the close on **2026-08-12** — their figures here are Q1 (April/March period ends) and must be re-scored tomorrow. **YSWY** has filed only one quarter as a public company (Q1, period ended 3/31/26); its Q2 10-Q is not yet on file. **SFM** does not disclose traffic vs ticket, so its comp composition is unverified. **IMKTA** and **YSWY** give no forward guidance, so the "guidance direction" test cannot be run on them.
- **Inferred (my arithmetic, not filing-stated):** Ingles' ex-insurance gross margin (~23.9%); ACI's pro-forma leverage on cut guidance (~2.47x); ARKO's ~$50M EBITDA sensitivity to a cpg reversion; GO's net debt of ~$431M; KR's ~+3.3% underlying ID sales after adding back the disclosed IRA/egg/generic drags.
- **Hypotheses explicitly tested and rejected on evidence:** SNAP/benefit changes (boilerplate risk-factor mentions only, in GO/ACI/KR; no company attributes results to it), GLP-1 drugs (**zero mentions across all nine filers**), input-cost inflation (contradicted by KR's "egg deflation," YSWY's +1.0% cost per unit, NGVC's mix/shrink/freight attribution), and fuel-margin collapse (contradicted — margins expanded at both c-store operators).
- **Scoring** is a weighted judgment across six factors — comp level *and* its traffic/ticket composition, gross-margin trend and its stated cause, guidance direction and stated reason, leverage and maturity profile, dividend/buyback safety, and niche defensibility — plus an information-quality penalty for stale or missing disclosure. It is directional, not a calibrated probability model.
