# Thesis 1 — Who Is Positioned to CAPTURE the AI Data-Center + Power Build-Out

*Ranked positioning probability from primary SEC filings (latest 10-Q / 10-K MD&A + earnings 8-K exhibits, Q2-2026 season). This scores operational POSITIONING — the size, growth, and data-center/power-leverage of real backlog plus the capacity to deliver it. It is NOT a price prediction and NOT advice. The 21d/63d price moves in the brief are context only and were not inputs. As of 2026-08-10.*

**Scoring:** 0–100% probability the firm is operationally well-positioned to capture the build-out, as a weighted read of five factors: (a) data-center/power revenue mix & backlog exposure; (b) total backlog + q/q & y/y growth + coverage; (c) latest guidance direction & reason; (d) margin trajectory; (e) balance sheet / capacity / skilled-labor. Factors (a) and (b) carry the most weight — the thesis rewards *growing, data-center-levered backlog you can actually build*.

## Evidence behind each score (verified vs inferred)

**1. PWR (Quanta Services) — 89%.** VERIFIED from the Q2 10-Q: RPO and total backlog were **$33.55B and $53.44B at 6/30/26, up 41.2% and 21.5% y/y**; the Electric segment (grid/transmission/substation + data-center electrical) backlog alone grew **$17.96B → $29.11B (+62% y/y)**. Revenue rose 41% to $9.56B and management is "increasing our full-year 2026 financial expectations across all metrics" (adj. EBITDA guided $4.09–4.21B). PWR is the only name that builds *both* legs of the thesis — the power/grid feeding data centers *and* the mission-critical facilities (Phalcon and Enerfab acquisitions target "the growing data center and mission-critical facility markets"). Its self-perform craft-labor base is the scarce resource the whole thesis hinges on. The one caution I VERIFIED: it priced **$2.0B of senior notes on Aug 4 (closing Aug 6)** for general corporate purposes/CP repayment ([notes 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1050915/000119312526331400/d114917dex991.htm)) — funding growth, but adding leverage. Ranks #1 on the widest, most complete, still-growing power-and-data-center book.

**2. EME (EMCOR) — 87%.** VERIFIED from the 10-Q: record RPO **$17.14B vs $11.91B y/y (+43.9%)** and $13.25B at year-end. The revenue-by-market-sector tables show **Network & Communications (data centers) is now 56–58% of U.S. construction-segment revenue** (up from ~50%) and "the largest increase was seen within the network and communications market sector." Q2 delivered a record **10.6% operating margin** and EPS $9.06 (+34.8%), and EMCOR **raised guidance for the second time** — revenue to $20.0–20.5B (from $18.5–19.25B), EPS to $32.00–33.25, operating margin to 9.5–9.8%. The honest offset, also VERIFIED: **high-tech (semiconductor-fab) construction is contracting** (17%→10% of mechanical revenue "as we completed certain semiconductor manufacturing construction projects"), so the growth engine is narrowing to data centers. Sits just below PWR because it's inside-the-building MEP/electrical rather than grid/power, but the double guidance-raise and record margins are the strongest forward evidence in the group.

**3. FIX (Comfort Systems) — 86%.** VERIFIED from the 10-Q: backlog **$14.06B, +73.1% y/y and +12.9% q/q** (same-store +68.7%) — the fastest backlog growth among the large caps — and MD&A states the growth is "**primarily... increased project bookings in the technology sector**" (Texas modular +$1.87B, Texas electrical +$1.13B) with demand "especially strong in the technology sector, particularly for data centers." Revenue +50.3% to $3.27B, gross margin expanding 23.5%→25.9%, and $189M of building purchases to grow the data-center modular business (capacity being added, VERIFIED). Ranks a hair below EME only because FIX gives **no numeric EPS guidance** (management is qualitatively "optimistic... well into 2027") and is slightly more concentrated in a handful of Texas mega-jobs. Its data-center attribution is the most explicit in the peer set.

**4. IESC (IES Holdings) — 83%.** VERIFIED from the Q3 (June) 8-K: total backlog **$4,525M vs $2,067M a year ago (+119%)** — the fastest growth in the group — with the data-center-levered Infrastructure Solutions segment up 73% "primarily in the data center end market" and Communications backlog $693M→$1,612M. Revenue +40% to $1,243M, operating income +60% (margins expanding), and the board approved a **2-for-1 split** — a confidence signal. Ranks below the big three because it is the smallest, more diversified (residential + C&I), and like IESC's practice, issues **no formal guidance**, so factor (c) is qualitative. On pure data-center backlog momentum it arguably rivals #1–3.

**5. AGX (Argan) — 78%.** VERIFIED from the Q1 (FY-Jan) 8-K: the most *direct* power-infrastructure play — a gas-fired power-plant EPC whose CEO ties demand explicitly to "the electrification of everything... and the proliferation of data centers... Gas-fired plants remain the ideal solution." Record revenue $291M (+50.2%), EPS $3.24 vs $1.60, gross margin 19%→21%, and a **pristine balance sheet (net cash, no debt)** with a new North Carolina fabrication facility under construction (capacity). The decisive knock, VERIFIED: **backlog is flat-to-declining — ~$3.0B → $2.9B (1/31) → $2.8B (4/30)** — as it burns work into record revenue faster than it books lumpy new gas-plant awards. I also confirmed the recent **Aug-4 8-K was only an $8.3M teledata tuck-in (ValCor), not a backlog award** ([8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/100591/000110465926090398/agx-20260804xex99d1.htm)), and its data is ~2 months old (next print ~Sept). Highest backlog *coverage* (~2.4x revenue) but the only top-half name whose backlog is shrinking — hence #5, not higher.

**6. HUBB (Hubbell) — 71%.** VERIFIED from the 8-K: CEO Bakker directly attributes results to the thesis — "Megatrends in **grid modernization, load growth and datacenter investment drove 10% organic growth**." Utility Solutions (grid) +10% and Electrical Solutions +25% (organic +18%, the data-center-power leg), and Hubbell **raised FY adjusted EPS to $20.25–20.55** (organic 9–11%). It drops to the second tier because it is a diversified *product* manufacturer with **no backlog to verify** growth against, **adjusted operating margin fell ~50 bps** (Electrical −130 bps) on tariffs/inflation/restructuring, and it flags "continued softness in the heavy industrial and residential markets." A high-quality picks-and-shovels grid/data-center supplier, but a step removed from "capturing" project backlog.

**7. PLPC (Preformed Line Products) — 62%.** VERIFIED from the 8-K: genuinely strong, record results — sales $212.7M (+25%), USA +32% "driven by robust demand in **energy markets**," record gross margin 34.3%, EPS $4.49 (+75%) — plus the May 2026 Delta Star acquisition to "accelerate growth in our U.S. **substation** business." Substation/grid hardware is physically close to the power build-out. But the data-center linkage is **INFERRED, not stated**: the release frames demand as "energy" and "communications" and never once says "data center." As a product company it discloses **no backlog and no guidance**, so three of five factors are thin. Ranks above ITRI because its grid-hardware sales are *growing* and margins are expanding.

**8. ITRI (Itron) — 55%.** The clearest price-vs-positioning gap. VERIFIED from the 8-K: despite a +22%/+28% price move, **revenue fell 7% to $563M**, **total backlog slipped to $4.4B from $4.5B**, and bookings of $550M imply a **book-to-bill below 1.0x**. The bull case is real but narrow — record adjusted gross margin 41.4% (+460 bps), ARR +21%, and "durable needs across grid expansion, resiliency" — a *margin/recurring-revenue re-rating*, not a data-center backlog capture story. Itron is grid-edge intelligence (meters, software), which benefits indirectly from load growth but does not *build* data-center or power capacity. Shrinking backlog + indirect leverage put it in the lower half despite excellent margins and a maintained FY guide ($2.37–2.41B rev, $6.30–6.50 EPS).

**9. LMB (Limbach) — 48%.** VERIFIED from the 8-K, and it is the sharpest cautionary tale: Limbach is *in* data-center MEP, but its own release blames the margin drop on "**competition for skilled labor and materials associated with construction activity in data center markets**" — i.e., it is a **price-taker being squeezed by** the build-out, not a beneficiary of it. Gross margin collapsed to **21.5% from 28.0%**, adjusted EBITDA −22%, and while it raised the *revenue* guide to $760–790M it **cut the gross-margin guide to 23–24% (from 26–27%)** and adj. EBITDA to $78–84M. Organic ODR revenue actually **fell 3.4%** (the +21.9% total is the Pioneer Power acquisition). Tiny ($173M/qtr), with only $17.5M cash against a drawn revolver. Exposed to the theme, positioned to capture revenue but not margin.

**10. MTRX (Matrix Service) — 35%, LOW confidence.** VERIFIED from the fiscal-Q3 8-K, but the data is **stale — as of 3/31/26, reported May 6** (June-FY filer; next print ~Sept). Even at face value it is the weakest fit: an LNG/storage/energy-infrastructure contractor with only "**more than $30 million**" of awards "related to... data center demand" inside a $1.0B backlog, a **0.5x book-to-bill**, a **cut** to FY revenue guidance (~$870M), and near-breakeven profitability ($0.8M net income, $0.03 EPS). Its lone strength is a clean balance sheet ($297M liquidity, no debt). Minimal, emerging data-center exposure + weak bookings + cut guidance + stale data = clear last, flagged low-confidence.

## Method + caveats

- **What this is.** A fundamental *positioning* probability read from primary filings — SEC EDGAR submissions API, the latest 10-Q/10-K MD&A, and earnings 8-K (EX-99.1) exhibits for the Q2-2026 season (fiscal-Q3 for IESC, fiscal-Q1 for AGX, fiscal-Q3 for MTRX). Every hard number above is quoted from those documents and linked. It is **not** a price forecast, a valuation call, or advice; "well-positioned" does not mean the stock will rise, and several of these names have already re-rated.
- **How scored.** Weighted read of five factors, with data-center/power backlog *size + growth* (factors a, b) weighted most heavily and guidance/margin/balance-sheet (c, d, e) supporting. Scores are spread deliberately: an elite, growing, verifiable data-center book (PWR/EME/FIX/IESC, 83–89) vs. direct-but-flat-backlog power (AGX, 78) vs. diversified suppliers with inferred/indirect linkage (HUBB/PLPC, 62–71) vs. shrinking-backlog or squeezed names (ITRI/LMB, 48–55) vs. minimal-exposure/stale (MTRX, 35).
- **Verification status.** All ten scores rest on VERIFIED primary-filing figures. Two linkages are explicitly INFERRED and were scored down for it: **PLPC**'s data-center connection (it discloses "energy"/substation demand, never "data center") and **MTRX**'s pipeline composition. **MTRX is additionally flagged LOW-confidence because its latest hard data is ~4 months old** (3/31/26); its June fiscal-year-end results were not yet filed as of this writing. **AGX** and **IESC** issue no numeric guidance, so their factor-(c) reads are qualitative. **HUBB**, **PLPC**, and **LMB** are product/bookings companies without a comparable project-backlog series.
- **The cross-cutting risk** for all: skilled craft labor is the shared bottleneck. It is a *moat* for those with self-perform scale (PWR, EME) and a *margin drain* for the sub-scale price-takers (LMB, explicitly). Watch backlog-growth direction next quarter — flat/declining books (AGX, ITRI, MTRX) are the earliest tell that demand capture is slowing.
