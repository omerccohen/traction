# Target Groups — Answering the Aug 11, 2026 Desk Note

*Research targets, grouped by value-chain role. Tickers come from the target-input dossier, the physical-proxy panel, the value-chain pulls, and keyword searches over the 2,992-name universe roster (`data_cache/universe/all_us_stocks.csv`) — none invented. Evidence is real web search, cited. VERIFIED = has a source URL; HYPOTHESIS = my inference. This is a "where to look and what to confirm" map, not a buy list.*

**The desk note's central claim — "sharply up on 21 days, still down on 63 days, so it is a bounce off a washout, not a confirmed up-cycle" — survives the evidence test, but not for the reason the note gives.** The 63-day hole is mostly an *equity* de-rating in mining/nuclear/energy shares, not a hole in the *commodity*. Copper and steel physicals are at or near record levels right now and were never washed out. What is genuinely absent is a **demand** turn: the drilling side of the chain is flat, and the metals tightness that does exist is supply-side and policy-made (tariffs, concentrate scarcity), not an end-demand acceleration. So: right conclusion, wrong mechanism, and the distinction changes which names are the research targets.

## Thesis 1 — The hard-commodity chain: is the +15.7%/21d oilfield-machinery move a demand turn, or is the scarcity somewhere else entirely?

**Thesis line.** The desk note asks "restocking and short-covering off a June–July washout, or a real turn in demand?" Reframed as one value chain — metal → mill → equipment OEM → distributor → service co → E&P operator — the answer is that the bottleneck sits at the **metal/mill end** and there is **no bottleneck at the drilling end**. The equipment equities re-rated on their own P&Ls, not on a cycle.

The desk note's own trigger (a second utility down ~20%) also stands, but check whether it is a pre-revenue reactor name before calling it sector contagion. **Tradability:** GFUZ (~$1.0M/day) and FISN (~$1.6M/day) are effectively untradable and FISN carries going-concern language; HDRN ~$0.1M/day; RNW ($3.5M/day) and CEPU ($3.7M/day) are thin. CEG, VST, AEP, NEE and OKLO all trade $0.5–1.1B/day and are heavily owned — nothing here is undiscovered.

## Thesis 3 — Food Chains: agriculture is flat, so what is actually breaking?

**Thesis line.** The desk note is right that this is not an input-cost story. It is a **volume and share** story: negative identical-sales across the whole cohort, with value-end share migrating to Walmart/Amazon/Aldi and a low-income consumer additionally squeezed at the fuel pump.
