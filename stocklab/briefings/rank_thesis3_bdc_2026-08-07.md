# Thesis 3 — Are private-credit marks durable? BDC book-quality ranking (as of 2026-08-07)

**Question being scored:** not price, not a buy call — *the probability each BDC's credit book stays healthy and its marks hold* (least likely to crack). Higher score = more durable book: low/stable non-accruals (read at **cost**, not just fair value), holding NAV, diversified, conservatively levered, dividend covered by NII.

**Data vintage:** Q2 2026 (quarter ended June 30, 2026) 10-Q + earnings 8-K for 7 of 8, pulled from SEC EDGAR. **BCSF is the exception — it has NOT reported Q2 yet (earnings call Aug 11, 2026); its figures below are Q1 2026 (March 31) and are flagged LOW-confidence/stale.**

**The skeptical lens applied throughout:** a low *fair-value* non-accrual sitting on top of a high or *rising cost-basis* non-accrual is a yellow flag, not a clean bill — it means loans have already gone bad and been marked down, and more cost basis is impaired than the FV headline shows.

## 2. Per-name evidence (VERIFIED = from the cited SEC filing; inferred = my calc)

**1. ARCC — 82% (Ares Capital).** The most durable book by construction: **619 portfolio companies, 273 PE sponsors, 59% first-lien**, weighted-average grade a steady 3.1, and ~$6.0B liquidity plus the sector's first commercial-paper program — idiosyncratic problems get absorbed. NII $0.50 covers the $0.48 dividend (104%) (VERIFIED, [8-K ex-99.1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1287750/000162828026050303/arccq2-2026exhibit991.htm)). The honest dings: non-accruals rose to **2.4% cost / 1.4% FV** (from 1.8%/1.2%), NAV slipped to **$19.35** (from $19.94 at YE25) and Q2 GAAP EPS was only **$0.24 vs $0.50 NII** — i.e., ~$0.26/sh of net markdowns — while net leverage ticked to **1.12x**, the top of its 0.9–1.25x range (VERIFIED). Still #1 because scale/diversification/liquidity make aggregate marks the hardest in the group to break, but this was not a pristine quarter.

**2. MAIN — 80% (Main Street).** NAV **rose +1.8% to $33.92** (from $33.33 at 3/31/26), DNII $1.04 covers the regular $0.795/qtr dividend **1.31x** (20th straight supplemental), and leverage is the group's lowest (**~0.7x**, asset coverage 244%) (VERIFIED, [10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1396440/000139644026000094/main-20260630.htm), [8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1396440/000139644026000090/main-q22026xearningsreleas.htm)). **The skeptical catch:** the headline 1.1% FV non-accrual sits on a **4.0% cost-basis non-accrual that rose from 3.3%** — $198.5M cost marked to $65.2M FV (~33¢) (VERIFIED, 10-Q MD&A). NAV strength also leans on LMM equity marked at **196% of cost** — a real cushion today but the mark most able to reverse in a downturn. Elite operator; ranked just behind ARCC because its NAV is more equity/mark-sensitive and its cost-basis impairments are quietly building.

**3. FDUS — 75% (Fidus).** Cleanest credit in the group: **one** portfolio company on non-accrual (Virtex, ~$0.2M FV / ~$11M cost, down from two), total portfolio marked at **102% of cost**, base dividend $0.43 covered by $0.50 adjusted NII (116%) with a fat **$1.00/sh spillover** cushion, NAV ~flat at $19.46, and conservative leverage (asset coverage 266%) (VERIFIED, [10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1513363/000119312526337982/fdus-20260630.htm), [8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1513363/000119312526337972/fdus-ex99_1.htm)). Held below the mega-caps only because it is a **lower-middle-market** lender — 100 companies, avg $14.0M, a **12.5% debt yield** that signals smaller/riskier borrowers — and the post-quarter **$11.0M Virtex realized loss** is a reminder that LMM names fail binary. Genuinely sound small-cap book.

**4. SLRC — 67% (SLR Investment).** Durability comes from collateral and granularity: a **$3.2B comprehensive portfolio across 860+ issuers, avg 0.1% each, 95.8% first lien**, with **98% of Q2 originations in defensive asset-based/specialty finance** (VERIFIED, [8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1418076/000117184326005207/exh_991.htm)). But the earnings/marks are softening: NII $0.33 barely covers the $0.31 distribution (1.06x), NAV slipped to **$18.00** (from $18.16) on $9.5M net losses, non-accruals are **2.8% cost vs 1.8% FV** (cost > FV), and management's own "rebuild the earnings power" language concedes the cash-flow book is under pressure. Solid collateral, weak current earnings.

**5. BCSF — 63% (Bain Capital Specialty Finance) — LOW-CONFIDENCE / STALE.** **BCSF has not reported Q2 2026** (earnings call Aug 11, 2026), so its marks are literally unverifiable as of this note. On **Q1 2026** data: diversified (**212 companies, 30 industries**), predominantly first lien, low non-accruals (**1.4% cost / 0.6% FV**, 6 names), 1.28x net leverage, dividend exactly covered (NII $0.42 = $0.42) (VERIFIED for Q1, [Q1 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1655050/000119312526217311/d845735dex991.htm)). The reason it is not higher: Q1 NAV **fell −2.1% to $16.86** and Q1 net income was only **$0.05/sh** — marks were already moving against it going into a quarter I cannot see. Book *structure* is good; *trajectory + information gap* cap the score. **Re-rank after 8/11.**

**6. NMFC — 57% (New Mountain Finance).** The improving-but-lower-quality book. Non-accruals fell to **1.5% FV** (from 2.6%) and NAV held at **$10.89** (VERIFIED, [8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1496099/000149609926000027/nmfcearningspressreleaseq2.htm)) — but only **63.3% is first lien** (the rest is SLP joint-venture funds, junior capital and equity), risk ratings show 13 Yellow / 7 Orange / 1 Red of 113 names, and adjusted NII $0.26 only thinly covers the $0.25 distribution. The tell is management's own stated priorities: **"reducing PIK income… selling certain equity positions… increasing portfolio diversification"** (VERIFIED, CEO quote) — i.e., they are describing a PIK-heavy, concentrated, equity-laden book they are actively trying to de-risk. Cost-basis non-accrual is not separately disclosed (transparency ding; FV-only figure treated as LOW-confidence on cost).

**7. CION — 52% (CION Investment).** Superficially improving — non-accruals down to **4.41% cost / 1.44% FV** (from 5.35%/1.53%), NAV up 3.5% to $13.57, "no new non-accruals or downgrades" (VERIFIED, [8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1534254/000110465926091692/tm2622057d1_ex99-1.htm)) — but the internals are weak. The **4.41% cost-basis non-accrual** is the second-highest here (marked to ~33¢), the NAV gain was **"primarily due to mark-to-market price increases to certain equity investments"** (equity marks, not credit — less durable), NII **$0.29 does not cover the $0.30 distribution** (0.97x), leverage is high at **1.52x net**, and the book is the least diversified (**82 companies, 23 industries**). The +20%/21d tape move is price, not book quality.

**8. MFIC — 45% (MidCap Financial).** The clearest deterioration. Non-accruals **rose to 4.6% cost / 2.8% FV** (from 3.9%/2.6%) — the highest and rising — NAV fell **−3.2% to $13.37** which management explicitly attributed to **"credit-related weakness concentrated in a limited number of positions,"** and net leverage is the group's highest at **1.54x** (VERIFIED, [10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1278752/000119312526335848/mfic-20260630.htm), [8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1278752/000119312526336509/d120983dex991.htm)). It is otherwise well-built (~229 companies, ~94% first lien) and the $0.31 dividend is covered on NII (1.29x) with accretive buybacks below NAV — but a diversified, first-lien book *still* cracking on credit is a cleaner deterioration signal, not a mitigant. Rising defaults + top-of-group leverage is the worst combination for mark durability.

## 3. Method & caveats

- **What this is:** a fundamental, filings-based *positioning* read on book durability. **Not a price prediction and not investment advice.** Nothing here says "buy" or "will go up." The 21d/63d price moves in the brief were context only and were not used to rank.
- **Sources:** SEC EDGAR submissions API + the actual Q2 2026 10-Q and earnings 8-K (exhibit 99.1) for each name, retrieved 2026-08-10 with a descriptive User-Agent. Every hard number above is quoted from those filings (linked inline). Figures labeled "inferred" are my arithmetic from filing data (e.g., ARCC's implied Q2 markdown = NII − GAAP EPS).
- **Cost vs fair value:** non-accruals are read at **amortized cost** as the primary lens, because a low FV figure can mask loans already written down. Rising cost-basis non-accruals flagged for MAIN (→4.0%), ARCC (→2.4%), MFIC (→4.6%); high cost basis flagged for CION (4.41%).
- **PIK creep:** explicitly flagged where management or the income statement shows it — NMFC (management prioritizing PIK reduction), MFIC (~$4.1M PIK, ~6% of investment income). Others were not separately quantified from the pulled sources and are not asserted.
- **LOW-confidence flags:** **BCSF** entirely (Q1 2026 data; Q2 unreported until 8/11/26 — re-rank then). **NMFC** cost-basis non-accrual (FV-only disclosure). ARCC Q1-to-Q2 NAV bridge is inferred, not directly disclosed.
- **Scoring** is a weighted judgment across the six factors (non-accrual level/trend at cost, NAV trend, diversification/first-lien %, leverage vs target, NII dividend coverage, PIK/junior-book tells); it is directional, not a calibrated probability model.
