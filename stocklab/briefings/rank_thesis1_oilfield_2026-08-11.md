# Thesis 1 — Oilfield Equipment: Is the Move Real Order Demand or Company Execution?

**Date:** 2026-08-11 (filings through Q2 2026, all filed July 21 – Aug 11 2026)
**Question tested:** "Oil and Gas Field Machinery" is the highest-attention field this week (+15.7%/21d). Is that strength backed by durable order demand, or by execution, mix, one-offs and cost cuts?

**Verdict: COMPANY EXECUTION, not a sector demand cycle.** The physical driver is negative, not flat. FET's own 10-Q reproduces the Baker Hughes rig table: **global active rigs 1,759 in Q2 2026 vs 1,832 in Q1 2026 and 1,777 in Q2 2025 — down both sequentially and year-over-year**; US oil rigs 420 vs 459 y/y ([FET 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1401257/000140125726000053/fet-20260630.htm)). US rig count sat at **588, unchanged**, on 2026-08-07 ([BOE Report](https://boereport.com/2026/08/07/us-energy-firms-leave-rig-count-unchanged-in-latest-week-baker-hughes-says/)), and Chevron set 2026 capex at **$18–19B, explicitly "at the low-end" of its $18–21B range** ([Chevron](https://www.chevron.com/newsroom/2025/q4/chevron-announces-2026-capex-budget-of-18-to-19-billion)).

The single cleanest test is inside **Baker Hughes**, which runs both businesses under one roof: the oilfield segment (OFSE) had **RPO flat sequentially at $3.0B, pressure-systems orders −4% y/y and EBITDA −11% y/y**, while the non-oilfield segment (IET — power generation, LNG, data centers) booked **$7.1B of orders, +101% y/y, book-to-bill 2.2x, backlog +19% to an all-time high**. Same company, same quarter: oilfield flat, power doubling. Where order demand in this field is genuinely booming, it is **not** oilfield demand.

A second cross-cutting artifact: **tariff refunds** flattered at least two names this quarter — NOV booked **~$40M** of them (more than its entire y/y EBITDA increase of $31M), and Cactus cited "reciprocal and fentanyl tariff-related refunds" in Pressure Control margins. That is a non-demand earnings tailwind that will not repeat.

## Company detail — verified vs inferred

**BKR — 87%.** VERIFIED from the 8-K EX-99.1: total orders $10,501M against $6,742M revenue (1.56x), IET orders $7,088M vs $3,530M a year ago, IET RPO $37.1B (+19% y/y, all-time high), IET book-to-bill 2.2x, and a *raised* order guidance with Horizon-2 IET orders lifted above $45B. This is the only name in the field where a raised guide is justified by the order book rather than by margins. It ranks first because no other company here shows both a book-to-bill above 1.5x and a rising multi-year backlog — but the honest caveat, also verified, is that the growth is entirely IET (power generation, LNG, data centers) while OFSE, the actual oilfield segment, has flat RPO, orders −4% y/y and EBITDA −11% y/y.

**OIS — 71%.** VERIFIED: backlog $451M, described by the company as its highest since March 2015, up 5% q/q, on Q2 bookings of $114M for a 1.2x book-to-bill and 1.1x year-to-date. The year-to-date figure matters — it means the order strength survived two quarters, not one, which is why OIS ranks above HMH and FTI. It ranks below BKR because the company itself discloses the quarter was "augmented by a significant contract award," the Offshore Manufactured Products segment's EBITDA and margin actually *declined* sequentially, and the absolute base is tiny ($19M group EBITDA), so a single award swings the ratio.

**HMH — 60%.** VERIFIED: orders $205M (+19% y/y), book-to-bill 1.2x, service orders +50% y/y. INFERRED (arithmetic from disclosed components): services $118M plus spares $65M is $183M of the $205M, so roughly 89% of intake is aftermarket and implied product orders are only ~$22M. That distinction is why it sits below OIS — installed-base aftermarket on regulated offshore equipment is durable revenue, but it is maintenance demand, not a new equipment cycle. Revenue −16% y/y with product revenue −65% y/y, and EBITDA +3% y/y credited to "cost optimization," place it firmly on the execution side of the line; it ranks above FTI only because its order intake is actually *growing*.

**FTI — 54%.** VERIFIED: backlog $16.44B but −1.2% y/y and −0.2% q/q, inbound orders −3.7% y/y, subsea book-to-bill exactly 1.0x, and Surface Technologies orders −21% y/y with backlog −27.4% y/y. Management attributes the 240bp margin expansion to "strong execution," and notably made **no change to guidance issued February 19** despite a large beat — a company seeing a demand inflection normally raises. It ranks above WHD because its backlog is vast, long-dated and provides real multi-year revenue durability; it ranks below HMH because at 1.0x book-to-bill it is replacing backlog, not building it, and its most rig-sensitive division is contracting sharply.

**WHD — 45%.** VERIFIED and decisive: remaining performance obligations fell from $537.5M at March 31 to $455.8M at June 30, a **15% sequential decline**, established by comparing the Q1 and Q2 10-Qs. Management's own guidance confirms the mechanism — Q3 consolidated revenue down, Pressure Control −10%, because Q2 "benefitted from strong backlog execution in Cactus International," helped further by pulled-forward Latin America deliveries and tariff refunds. It still ranks mid-table, not bottom, because Spoolable Technologies is a genuinely order-led business (guided +15–20% q/q, Baytown capacity added explicitly "to meet increased global demand," and >$130M of post-quarter international purchase orders). The +35%/21d move, though, rests substantially on a quarter that consumed backlog rather than built it.

**INVX — 41%.** VERIFIED: revenue $244.9M (+9% y/y), adjusted EBITDA $48.0M (+3% y/y) with margin down to 20% from 21%, and a disclosed $20M subsea award in Malaysia. NOT VERIFIABLE: Innovex discloses no backlog and no book-to-bill, so its "commercial momentum" claim cannot be checked against an aggregate order number — under the rule that an unverifiable name ranks below a verified one, that caps it here. It sits below WHD because WHD at least publishes an RPO figure (even an unflattering one), and above FLOC because INVX names specific, quantified awards while its Q3 guide-up is explicitly inflated by the TCO acquisition that closed July 1.

**FLOC — 39%.** VERIFIED: revenue $235.9M (+22% y/y) but Natural Gas Technologies exactly flat y/y at $65.0M, margin down sequentially to 39.8% from 40.8%, and results only "within our original guidance range." No backlog, no book-to-bill and no forward numeric guidance are disclosed at all. Its genuine merit — production optimization on existing wells is structurally insensitive to the rig count and generates recurring rental revenue — is a durability argument, not an order-demand argument. It ranks below INVX because it names no quantified awards and its growth is Valiant-acquisition-fed, and above DNOW because its margin structure is real rather than synergy-derived; note it remains −12% over 63 days, so there is little sustained strength to explain.

**DNOW — 36%.** VERIFIED: revenue $1,307M vs $628M y/y, a doubling that is the MRC Global acquisition, not demand; adjusted EBITDA +54% q/q attributed to "integration and cost management initiatives"; record $133M operating cash flow explicitly sourced to "strong collections" and "inventory streamlining" (a working-capital release); and a **GAAP net loss of $21M**. The real positive, also verified, is non-rig-sensitive volume: US midstream revenue crossed $1B annualized for the first time and gas utility revenue posted its strongest sequential growth since 2022. It ranks below FLOC because as a distributor it offers zero order visibility, issued no guidance, and its earnings improvement is predominantly merger synergy — yet its +28%/21d and +27%/63d move is among the field's largest.

**NOV — 31%.** VERIFIED: book-to-bill **74%**, Energy Equipment backlog $4.08B, down $220M y/y — confirming the prior research exactly. The critical find is that adjusted EBITDA "+$31M y/y" **includes approximately $40M of tariff refunds**, so underlying EBITDA declined year-over-year; profit gains are credited to "strong execution on offshore production projects nearing completion" and "favorable sales mix," and Q3 EBITDA is guided to $240–270M against $283M delivered. It ranks above WFRD because its order intake did at least grow (+13% y/y, book-to-bill improving from 66% to 74%) and its offshore awards are specific and real. It ranks below DNOW because a sub-1.0 book-to-bill with a shrinking backlog is direct evidence *against* order demand, whereas DNOW simply has no order data.

**WFRD — 27%.** VERIFIED: revenue −8% y/y, operating income −55% y/y, net income −71% y/y, adjusted EBITDA −12% y/y, and — most decisive — "our total year outlook has **slightly reduced**." It is the only company in the field to cut guidance. Every favourable item is a cost or cash lever rather than demand: free cash flow +76% y/y achieved partly through capex −22%, $15M of NCS Multistage cost synergies, and $20–30M of annual cash savings from the Delaware redomestication. The MPD awards from Noble, Constellation, Ventura and Valaris are genuine but unquantified, and no backlog or book-to-bill is disclosed. It ranks above FET only because its +16%/21d move is modest and it is still −8% over 63 days, so far less price action requires explanation.

**FET — 25%.** The most important finding in this review, and the reason the field's biggest winner ranks last among names with positive moves. VERIFIED from the 10-Q's inbound-orders table: total orders **$235.9M in Q2 2026 versus $263.1M in Q2 2025, −10.3% y/y**, with Drilling & Completions orders **−18.8% y/y**. The advertised "104% book-to-bill" is therefore a *deterioration* from the 132% implied by prior-year orders against prior-year revenue — context the press release omits. FET's own MD&A states demand is "highly correlated with the global drilling rig count," then prints a table showing global rigs down both sequentially and year-over-year. Management attributes results to 13% market-share growth, "cost management," "facility consolidation" and plant utilization, and the raised guide implies EBITDA +40% on revenue +13% — a margin story, with the "strong" subsea orders being aftermarket ROV upgrades. Share gains in a shrinking market are real and can persist, but a **+64%/21d, +48%/63d** move against a **−10% y/y order book** is the widest price-versus-demand gap in the field.

**SEI — 15%.** CONFIRMED: Solaris Energy Infrastructure is a power and data-center infrastructure company carrying legacy SIC code 3533 from its Solaris Oilfield Infrastructure past. Verified from the Q2 10-Q segment tables: Power Solutions is 72% of revenue, 80% of segment EBITDA and **99.3% of capital expenditure**, with contracted capacity reaching ~3,200 MW by end-2029, an 18-year 660 MW turnkey contract for AI workloads, a 640 MW agreement with an affiliate of an investment-grade global technology company, and Stateline's ~900 MW data-center project. Its only oilfield segment, Solaris Logistics, shrank 17% y/y. It ranks last strictly because the question does not apply: there is no recent share-price strength to explain (−12%/21d, −21%/63d), and any conclusion drawn from it about oilfield demand would be an artifact of misclassification. **Recommendation: exclude SEI from this field's aggregate statistics** — it is a power name, and its presence inflates the field's apparent dispersion.

## Method + caveats

**Method.** Every hard number above comes from a primary SEC filing retrieved directly from EDGAR (submissions API → filing index → EX-99.1 earnings exhibit or the 10-Q itself), fetched via `curl`/`urllib` with a descriptive User-Agent and parsed locally. Q2 2026 10-Qs and earnings 8-Ks for all twelve companies were filed between 2026-07-21 and 2026-08-11 and were all available. Scores weight: (1) book-to-bill level **and** its year-over-year direction, (2) backlog level plus y/y and q/q change, (3) what management explicitly attributes results to, (4) guidance direction and its stated reason, (5) end-market mix, with an explicit penalty for identifiable one-offs (tariff refunds, working-capital releases, pull-forwards, M&A-inflated y/y comparisons) and for absent order disclosure.

**Confidence flags.**
- **LOW confidence — no order metric exists to verify:** **INVX, FLOC, DNOW, WFRD** disclose neither backlog nor book-to-bill in their Q2 releases. Their scores are capped in the 27–41% band for that reason alone, per the rule that an unverifiable name ranks below a verified one. If any of them publishes an order figure, these ranks should be revisited first.
- **WHD backlog trend is inferred from two filings**, not from a company-stated comparison: $537.5M (Q1 10-Q) versus $455.8M (Q2 10-Q). The −15% q/q is arithmetic on two verified disclosures, but Cactus itself never frames it that way.
- **HMH's ~89% aftermarket share of orders is arithmetic** on disclosed components ($118M services + $65M spares of $205M), not a company-stated split.
- **Not verified:** I did not obtain earnings-call transcripts or Q&A for any name — all management attribution above is quoted from written releases and MD&A, which are more conservative than call commentary. Analyst estimates, revisions and positioning data were not consulted; nothing here speaks to what was already priced in. The 21d/63d price moves were supplied as context and were not independently re-verified.
- **NOV's ~$40M tariff refund** is company-disclosed in a footnote to its own EBITDA highlight; the split ($14M Energy Equipment, $26M Energy Products & Services) is also disclosed. The inference that underlying EBITDA fell y/y is mine, from $283M − $40M = $243M versus $252M a year earlier.

**Scope.** This is a positioning and evidence-quality assessment of whether reported strength is order-backed. It is not a price prediction, not a valuation judgement, and not a recommendation to transact in any security.
